# Meta-analysis in English Language Education

## History, Characteristics, and Significance

### 1. What is meta-analysis

Meta-analysis is a research method that combines the results of multiple individual studies by converting them into a common statistical index called an effect size (Glass, 1976; APA Dictionary of Psychology, 2018).

Unlike a traditional literature review, which mainly summarizes previous studies descriptively, meta-analysis aims to examine the overall size and direction of an effect across studies and to identify the conditions under which the effect may vary (Borenstein et al., 2009).

In this sense, meta-analysis is not simply a summary of prior research. It is a quantitative method for building cumulative knowledge from many separate studies.

Online link:
- APA Dictionary of Psychology, "Meta-analysis": https://dictionary.apa.org/meta-analysis

### 2. History of meta-analysis

Meta-analysis is commonly traced back to Glass (1976), who introduced the term to distinguish it from primary analysis and secondary analysis. His article is widely regarded as the formal starting point of meta-analysis as a distinct research approach.

After that, meta-analysis spread quickly across psychology, medicine, and education. In educational research, it became especially important as a way to synthesize findings from many studies on teaching methods, feedback, and learning outcomes (Glass, 1976; Hattie, 2009).

A well-known example in education is Hattie (2009), whose large-scale synthesis showed how meta-analysis can influence educational theory, practice, and policy.

### 3. Main characteristics of meta-analysis

Meta-analysis has several important characteristics.

First, it uses effect sizes, which make it possible to compare and combine studies that use different samples, instruments, and research designs (Borenstein et al., 2009).

Second, it can examine moderator variables. This means researchers can go beyond asking whether an intervention works and ask under what conditions it works better or worse. For example, they can compare effects by learner age, proficiency level, treatment duration, or feedback type (Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

Third, meta-analysis can provide more stable and cumulative conclusions than a single study because it synthesizes evidence from many studies rather than relying on one local result (Borenstein et al., 2009).

### 4. Significance of meta-analysis in education

Education is a field in which many variables interact, including learners, teachers, curriculum, and learning contexts. Because of this complexity, studies on the same teaching method often report different results.

Meta-analysis is valuable in education because it helps researchers determine whether a given instructional approach is generally effective across many studies (Hattie, 2009). It therefore provides broader evidence than a single study and supports evidence-based decision making in teaching, curriculum design, and educational policy (Hattie, 2009; Borenstein et al., 2009).

In other words, meta-analysis is important not only as a statistical method but also as a practical tool for educational judgment.

### 5. Why meta-analysis is important in English language education

English language education and second language acquisition research are highly diverse in research setting and design. Studies differ in learner age, first language, proficiency, learning environment, target skill, assessment method, and treatment duration.

Because of this diversity, findings from individual studies may seem inconsistent or even contradictory. Meta-analysis is especially useful in this field because it helps synthesize heterogeneous studies and identify both the overall effectiveness of instructional treatments and the conditions under which their effects differ (Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

In second language research, meta-analysis has become an important methodological tool because it allows researchers to move beyond isolated findings and develop broader conclusions about language teaching and learning.

### 6. Major contributions of meta-analysis in English language education

One of the most influential meta-analyses in the field is Norris and Ortega (2000). Their study synthesized research on second language instruction and showed that L2 instruction has a generally positive effect, while also demonstrating that the size of the effect depends on factors such as instructional type and outcome measures.

This study is often regarded as a landmark because it provided quantitative evidence that instruction matters in second language learning and helped establish meta-analysis as a central method in SLA research (Norris & Ortega, 2000; Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

Another important contribution comes from Plonsky and Oswald (2014), who argued that effect sizes in L2 research should not always be interpreted using general guidelines such as Cohen's benchmarks. Instead, they suggested that L2 research needs field-specific standards for understanding how large or small an effect really is.

This point is important because it shows that meta-analysis in English language education is not just a technical procedure. It also requires interpretation that reflects the characteristics of the field itself.

### 7. Practical significance in English language education

In English language education, teachers and researchers often want to know whether a teaching method, digital tool, pronunciation treatment, or AI-based feedback system is truly effective.

Individual studies can provide useful local evidence, but they are often limited to a particular group of learners and a specific instructional context. Meta-analysis helps integrate these studies and identify what tends to work overall and under what conditions it works better (Norris & Ortega, 2000; Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

This makes meta-analysis especially valuable in teacher education, materials development, curriculum design, and policy discussions. In a field where methods and technologies change quickly, meta-analysis helps distinguish temporary trends from more stable evidence.

### 8. Limitations and cautions

Meta-analysis is powerful, but it does not automatically guarantee objectivity.

If the quality of the included studies is low, the overall result may also be weak or misleading. In addition, decisions about study selection, coding, effect-size calculation, and treatment of heterogeneity can all influence the final outcome (Borenstein et al., 2009; Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

For this reason, the value of a meta-analysis depends on transparent procedures, clear inclusion criteria, reliable coding, appropriate statistical methods, and careful interpretation.

### 9. Summary

To sum up, meta-analysis is a method for building cumulative knowledge by combining research findings through effect sizes (Glass, 1976). In education, it serves as a way to examine the effectiveness of instructional interventions more systematically (Hattie, 2009). In English language education, it is especially important because it helps synthesize highly diverse studies and provides evidence that can support both research and practice (Norris & Ortega, 2000; Oswald & Plonsky, 2010).

For this reason, meta-analysis has become one of the key methodological foundations for connecting research findings to practical decisions in English language education.
