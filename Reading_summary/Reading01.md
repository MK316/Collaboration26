# 🌱 Reading #1: Li (2018) Translanguaging as a Practical Theory

Li, W. (2018). Translanguaging as a practical theory of language. Applied Linguistics, 39(1), 9–30. https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amx039


## 1. Introduction: Beyond "Sloganization"
+ Status: Captures imagination across disciplines (pedagogy, arts, music).
+ Problem: Terminological confusion/fashionable label vs. theoretical depth.
+ Argument: Translanguaging is a robust practical theory challenging traditional views on Language & Thought and Modularity of Mind.
+ Core Value: Explains the fluid, multimodal nature of human communication.

## 2. Applied Linguistics & Practical Theory
+ Field Gap: AL often "borrows" concepts; seen as atheoretical/lower scientific value.
+ Practical Theory Defined: * Source: Dialectical materialism (Mao/Marx); Practice -> Theory -> Practice loop.
  + Descriptive Adequacy: Prioritizes richness/depth over formal "elegance."
  + Goal: Holistic interpretation to ask better questions, not just solve problems.
+ Candidate: Translanguaging moves from describing systems to analyzing actual user practices.

## 3. 21st Century Language Practice
+ Evidence: 

1. New Chinglish: Creative acts expressing sociopolitical subtext. 
    + Niubility = Niubi(Chinese: awesome) + Ability: Exceptional capability or awesomeness in a blend of genuine admiration and playful mockery tone.
    + Democrazy = Democracy + Crazy
    + How old are you? (Why always you? Zěnme lǎo shì nǐ?)

2. Singaporean Multilingualism: Fluid interweaving of regional varieties and English.
    + Observation: Six different languages blending instantly and fluidly in actual speech.
    + Key Insight: Meaning-making matters more than following the "rules" of a single language system.
    + Takeaway: We should stop chasing the myth of "pure" language and instead value the rich, personalized way (idiolect) that multilingual individuals actually communicate.

+ Key Shifts:
  + Post-Multilingualism: Simply "having" many languages is insufficient.
  + Myth of Purity: Mixing is vital to language evolution, not "contamination."
  + Boundary Breaking: Focus on how speakers navigate semiotic realities.

## 4. Why Translanguaging? (Shift in Perspective)
+ Pedagogy -> Process: From Welsh classroom strategy to a theory of meaning-making.
+ The "**Trans**-" Prefix:
  +  Trans-Language: Multilinguals use an Idiolect (personal repertoire), not "named" languages.
  + Trans-Modal: Human thought is multimodal (gestures, visuals, sounds).
+ Challenging Myths:
  + Anti-Modularity: No "language-specific" brain region; language is a multisensory system.
  + Language of Thought: We think in an idiolect, not in the artificial boundaries of a nation-state language.

## 5. Translanguaging Space & Instinct
#### 5.1 Space (The "Where"):

+ Thirdspace: A site of openness where macro/micro and social/psychological dichotomies break down.
+ Function: Combines personal history and capacity into a coordinated performance.

#### 5.2 Instinct (The "How/Why"):
+ Principle of Abundance: Innate drive to use all cues (sound, gaze, gesture).
+ Multi-competence: Learners don't just "acquire" code; they adapt bodies/brains to participate.

## 6. Conclusion: The Added Values
+ Reconceptualization: Language naming is political/ideological; "Which language?" is an irrelevant question.
+ Three Pillars of "Trans-":
  1. Transcending: Moving beyond socially constructed systems.
  2. Transformative: Changing individual cognition and social structures.
  3. Transdisciplinary: Integrating linguistics, psychology, sociology, and education.
+ Final Goal: **_Understanding the human mind as a holistic multi-competence_**.
