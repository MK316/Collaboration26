## Chapter 1: History and ‘definitions’
- focusing on the epistemological and practical foundations of Applied Linguistics (AL).


### 1. The Challenge of Definition
Defining Applied Linguistics is difficult because it "means many things to many people". Davies (2007) identifies two primary ways the field is defined:

• **The Ostensive (외연적) Approach**: This defines the field by looking at what its professionals actually do—the "look around you" method. In this view, AL is a "cover term" for a group of semi-autonomous disciplines, such as sociolinguistics and psycholinguistics, that each develop their own methodologies.
• ****The Dictionary (or Core) Approach: Proponents like Widdowson and Guy Cook argue for a coherent core, defining AL as the "academic discipline concerned with the relation of knowledge about language to decision making in the real world". This view sees the task of AL as mediating between linguistics and language use.

### 2. Source vs. Target: What is being Applied?
A major question in the field is whether the "source" of AL is linguistics alone or a broader synthesis.

• **The Source**: While linguistics is essential, AL must also draw on psychology, sociology, education, and measurement theory.
• **The Target**: Rather than being the "science of everything," many scholars narrow the target to language teaching (broadly including speech therapy, translation, and language planning) to maintain focus.
• **Brumfit’s Definition**: AL is the "theoretical and empirical investigation of real-world problems in which language is a central issue". These problems include success, failure, ability, disability, and ethical or gender issues.

### 3. Historical Evolution and Scope
The field has evolved from a narrow "application of linguistics" to a more "catholic view" (보편적 관점) of language studies.

• **Institutional Shift** (제도적 변화): In the 1990s, the journal Language Learning acknowledged the need for diverse methodologies including ethnography, cognitive science, and cultural studies. Some scholars even argued for dropping the label "applied linguistics" because it was seen as an ad hoc label for academic respectability.
• **Broadening Scope**: While AL began in the 1950s focused on language teaching (specifically TEFL), it has expanded into institutional language use, politics, and "critical applied linguistics," which explores identity, sexuality, and ethics.

### 4. Professionalization and Training
Applied Linguistics typically functions as a postgraduate qualification.

• Student Profile: Unlike other applied sciences, students are often admitted to AL programs without undergraduate training in linguistics; instead, they bring professional experience in teaching, journalism, or translation.
• Core Curriculum: A survey of global institutions shows a standard core: linguistic analysis, research methods, sociolinguistics, SLA (Second Language Acquisition), and psycholinguistics. Every institution surveyed also included at least one course with "teaching" in its title.

### 5. Status Among "Applied" Disciplines
AL shares a struggle for academic status common to other applied fields like applied mathematics, physics, and anthropology.

• The Theory-Practice Tension: There is a recurring tension between "pure" (general) linguistics and applied outcomes.
• Permanent Problems: Unlike "pure" sciences seeking finality, AL deals with recurring problems—such as the optimum age for L2 learning or treating memory loss—that may only find local, temporary solutions.

### 6. A Reversal of Roles: AL as the "Driver"
The author concludes with a provocative "unorthodox suggestion": Applied Linguistics may actually be the driver of linguistics.

• **The "Pole Position":** Practical socio-political and economic imperatives (like the need for an educated class) drive speculation and "pure research," rather than the other way around.
• The Dynamic: AL seeks out and works on real-world language problems; linguistics then responds by idealizing and analyzing these problems in terms of current theory.
• _Examples_: Fields like SLA, error analysis, and critical discourse analysis all began as practical applied concerns before being absorbed into "theoretical" linguistics.
